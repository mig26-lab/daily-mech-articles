# Agile Robots Raises $500 Million at $8.5 Billion Valuation in SoftBank-Led Round

**Published: November 14, 2026**  
**Category: Robotics / AI / Funding**

![Humanoid robot hand technology](https://cdn.pixabay.com/photo/2025/04/28/15/08/ai-9565202_640.jpg)

*Image: AI robot hand technology from Pixabay (Pixabay Content License)*

Munich-based Agile Robots closed a $500 million Series F funding round on November 12, 2026, valuing the Physical AI company at $8.5 billion. SoftBank Group led the round, with participation from NVIDIA, Hitachi Group (including Hitachi Ventures), T.Capital, and others. The raise brings total funding to approximately $2 billion.

## Company Background and Scale

Founded in 2018 as a spin-off from the German Aerospace Center (DLR), Agile Robots develops humanoid and mobile robots, robotic arms and hands, and the AI software that powers them. The company reports more than 20,000 robotic solutions installed worldwide across automotive, consumer electronics, healthcare, and service industries. It holds over 650 patents and employs more than 2,000 R&D specialists.

Earlier in 2026, Agile opened a production site in Fürstenfeldbruck near Munich with capacity for up to 10,000 legged and wheeled humanoid robots annually. The company has positioned itself as Europe's most valuable privately held Physical AI robotics firm, ahead of peers such as Neura Robotics.

## Use of Proceeds

Funds will support expansion in the United States, additional production capacity (including in Germany), and entry into new markets. CEO and founder Zhaopeng Chen has emphasized the company's vision of intelligent robots working alongside people in factories and warehouses. The capital also strengthens potential future IPO considerations, according to some reporting.

## Market Context

The funding arrives amid a wave of capital into humanoid and Physical AI companies. SoftBank has previously backed Agile, including a 2021 Series C that valued it above $1 billion. NVIDIA's participation aligns with its broader investments in robotics simulation, software, and hardware ecosystems.

Agile's growth reflects accelerating commercial deployments of humanoids beyond demos into real industrial settings. Challenges remain around reliability, cost, and scaling production, but large rounds like this signal investor confidence in the trajectory toward practical Physical AI.

## Broader Implications

This valuation and investor syndicate underscore Europe's rising role in robotics alongside US and Chinese players. Integration of frontier AI models with advanced hardware is a key differentiator. Partnerships and technology collaborations (including with major AI labs) will be important as production ramps.

For the industry, continued capital inflow supports the transition from prototypes to high-volume manufacturing of general-purpose and specialized robots.
