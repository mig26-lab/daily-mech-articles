# Swiss Robotics Day Highlights Shift Toward Dexterity: Unitree Hand and Inveel Skin

**Published: November 14, 2026**  
**Category: Robotics / Physical AI / Dexterity**

![Robotic hand with advanced sensors](https://cdn.pixabay.com/photo/2018/12/03/12/03/hand-prosthesis-3853281_640.jpg)

*Image: Robotic hand visualization from Pixabay (Pixabay Content License)*

Swiss Robotics Day 2026 took place on November 13 in Zurich, drawing roughly 170 robots and a strong focus on manipulation rather than locomotion. After years of progress on walking and balance, exhibitors concentrated on grasping, force modulation, and tactile sensing. Two standout demonstrations illustrated the current push toward finer physical intelligence.

## Unitree’s High-DoF Hand

Wang Xingxing, founder and CEO of Unitree, presented a new robot hand roughly the size of a human hand with more than 20 degrees of freedom. Higher degrees of freedom allow individual finger joints to move more independently, improving the ability to grasp irregular objects and adjust grip posture in real time. The design reportedly incorporates structures that help absorb impact between the fingers and the palm. Unitree also displayed its broader lineup of quadrupeds and humanoids, reinforcing the company’s emphasis on both mobility and now more capable end-effectors.

## Inveel’s Ultra-Thin Robot Skin

Swiss startup Inveel showed a tactile skin only 500 micrometers thick. A single thin sensor detects pressure and contact while also sensing an object’s approach from up to 15 centimeters away. It can register forces as small as 0.0005 newtons—well below what human fingertips typically feel. On stage, a robot hand using tactile feedback adjusted its grip in real time as an object began to slip, curling the fingers again just before the item would have fallen. The thin form factor makes the skin suitable for conformal application on existing hands and arms without adding significant bulk.

Other component suppliers, including Sonceboz with lightweight high-torque actuators for fingers and Bota Systems with force/torque sensing platforms, reinforced the theme that reliable hardware is required to turn learned policies into industrial performance.

## Implications for Physical AI

Humanoid and general-purpose robots are moving from demonstration walking into tasks that demand reliable contact. Factory kitting, assembly, and material handling all require consistent force control and the ability to recover from small slips or variations. The combination of high-degree-of-freedom hands and sensitive proximity-plus-contact skins addresses two long-standing bottlenecks: the mechanical capability to shape a grasp and the sensory feedback needed to close the loop in real time.

Swiss Robotics Day, organized by ETH Zurich RobotX with support from the Swiss Robotics Association, continues to serve as a meeting point for academia, startups, and established suppliers. The 2026 edition’s emphasis on hands and touch suggests that the next wave of competitive differentiation may lie as much in end-effectors and sensors as in the legs and balance systems that dominated earlier years.
