# SpaceX Plans Over $74 Billion in AI Supercomputing Campuses in Texas and Arizona

**Published: November 14, 2026**  
**Category: AI / Infrastructure / Tech**

On November 12, 2026, SpaceX announced plans to invest more than $74 billion in two major AI supercomputing campuses: over $24 billion at its Starbase site in Texas and roughly $50 billion in Cochise County, Arizona. The projects expand the company's computing infrastructure as its AI business has grown to generate more than half of revenue in recent quarters.

## Project Details

The Texas facility, on SpaceX-owned land near Brownsville / Starbase (approximately 444 acres), will train frontier AI models and generate its own electricity via an on-site gas-fired power plant (roughly 708–748 MW capacity), avoiding reliance on the public grid. It is projected to support more than 500 permanent jobs and has already contributed to thousands of construction positions. SpaceX is also investing $220 million in local water and wastewater infrastructure.

The Arizona campus (about 747 acres, nine buildings) targets up to 6 GW of capacity at full build-out. It will connect to local electric cooperatives, incorporate significant battery storage (reported around 24 GWh), and use dry cooling to minimize water use. It is expected to create about 5,500 construction jobs and 1,000 permanent roles. Both projects face local approval processes, with key votes scheduled around December 9.

## Strategic Context

SpaceX has evolved beyond rockets and Starlink into a major AI compute player. The self-powered Texas design and cooperative partnership in Arizona reflect different approaches to the energy and community challenges of gigawatt-scale AI training clusters. The company has highlighted potential benefits such as job creation and, in Arizona, possible reductions in bills for cooperative members.

These campuses position SpaceX to supply internal workloads and potentially external customers amid surging global demand for AI training and inference capacity. Local opposition and environmental reviews remain factors, particularly around power, water, and land use.

## Industry Impact

Massive private investments in dedicated AI supercomputing highlight the capital intensity of frontier AI development. Off-grid and hybrid power strategies may become more common as grid constraints grow. For the broader tech ecosystem, additional high-density compute capacity supports continued model scaling and deployment.

Approval outcomes and construction timelines will determine how quickly this capacity comes online. The plans underscore how AI infrastructure is now driving multi-tens-of-billions-dollar projects from companies traditionally known for other domains.
