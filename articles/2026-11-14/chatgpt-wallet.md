# OpenAI Launches ChatGPT Wallet: AI Agents Can Now Make Purchases with Biometric Approval

**Published: November 14, 2026**  
**Category: AI / Tech / Agentic Commerce**

On November 13, 2026, OpenAI introduced ChatGPT Wallet, a new feature that allows its AI agents—called “dots”—to complete purchases on behalf of users. Built in partnership with American Express, Mastercard, and Visa, the system prioritizes security by ensuring agents never access actual card details and requiring explicit user approval for every transaction.

## How ChatGPT Wallet Works

Users add an eligible card once. When a dot agent identifies a purchase (for example, after researching a product or filling a cart), the user reviews the items, total, and selected card. Approval requires a biometric check such as Face ID or a fingerprint scan. This creates a clear record of authorization.

After approval, the payment network issues a network token—a substitute number with its own security code and expiration—that the merchant receives. Neither the agent nor the business sees the real card details. Users retain standard card rewards and dispute protections.

The feature is initially available only through dots conversations (not standard ChatGPT chats or other interfaces) for ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the United States who are 18 or older and hold eligible US-issued cards. It is rolling out gradually so OpenAI can gather real-world feedback and improve reliability with banks and merchants.

## Agentic Commerce Context

This launch advances agentic AI into everyday commerce. Previously, tools like Instant Checkout required more manual setup or merchant integrations. Wallet lowers friction while maintaining user control—every purchase still needs human confirmation. OpenAI has indicated that future versions may support standing instructions or limited autonomous spending within set parameters (e.g., categories or spending limits).

The timing coincides with broader industry moves: competitors and partners are also exploring agent-driven shopping, and the partnership with major card networks signals institutional buy-in for tokenized, agent-mediated payments.

## Security and User Control

OpenAI emphasizes that Wallet is “the first wallet that lets agents pay without access to users’ actual card details and that requires a security check for every purchase.” By design, it avoids storing full card numbers and relies on network tokens. Early availability is restricted to learn from usage and refine compatibility.

Potential risks include over-reliance on agent recommendations or errors in product selection, but the mandatory per-transaction approval provides a strong safeguard. Users remain in the loop for review and biometric confirmation.

## Broader Implications

ChatGPT Wallet could accelerate the shift toward AI agents handling routine tasks like shopping, bookings, and bill payments. For businesses, it opens new channels for agent-accessible commerce via plugins or WebMCP tools. For users, it promises convenience once trust and accuracy mature.

As agent capabilities grow (with models like GPT-6 variants and multi-agent systems), secure payment rails become essential infrastructure. This release positions OpenAI at the intersection of AI assistants and financial systems.
