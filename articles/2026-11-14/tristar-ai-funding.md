# Tristar AI Raises $12.4 Million Series A to Scale Real-Time Factory Defect Detection

**Published: November 14, 2026**  
**Category: AI / Robotics / Manufacturing**

On November 12, 2026, Cambridge, Massachusetts-based Tristar AI announced a $12.4 million Series A funding round led by Isogon Ventures. Additional participants included Blackhorn Ventures, Ford Street Ventures, ISAI Cap Venture (Capgemini’s venture fund), Las Olas VC, NextView Ventures, Seraph Partners, SNR Ventures, and TenOneTen. The company builds a real-time AI visual inspection platform that detects defects on manufacturing lines as products move through production.

## How the Platform Works

Unlike traditional machine-vision systems that rely on fixed rules, Tristar’s models adapt across different products, defect types, and changing production conditions. The system integrates with existing cameras, programmable logic controllers, sensors, robotics, manufacturing execution systems, and enterprise resource planning platforms. Operators receive alerts within seconds, while supervisors gain visibility across multiple lines and facilities. The platform connects product quality data with machine performance and operator activity to help identify not only when a problem occurs but why.

Live deployments report defect-detection accuracy between 95% and 99%. Customers say the system typically pays for itself in about four months. Tristar currently monitors more than 500 production lines across North America and Europe, inspecting products valued in the hundreds of billions of dollars. Clients include major suppliers to Toyota, Honda, and Mercedes, as well as companies representing more than half of the plastics and film market.

## Company Background and Use of Funds

Founder and CEO Salem Karani has described how experiences in his family’s plastic-film plant shaped the company’s focus on catching costly errors early. The new capital will support further development of the AI vision and edge-computing platform, expansion into additional manufacturing sectors, and growth of customer-success, deployment, sales, and marketing teams to handle multi-site enterprise rollouts.

## Industry Context

Quality escapes remain expensive in high-volume manufacturing. A single undetected defect can generate scrap, rework, warranty claims, or customer rejections that erase months of margin. Adaptive AI inspection offers a way to reduce that risk without requiring a full replacement of existing camera and control infrastructure. Tristar’s funding round reflects continued investor interest in practical industrial AI applications that deliver measurable returns on the factory floor.

As more manufacturers seek to protect margins while coping with labor shortages and tighter quality standards, real-time visual intelligence layers are likely to see wider adoption. Tristar’s traction across automotive suppliers and packaging producers positions it to expand further in those and adjacent verticals.
