# Tesla Continues Early Production of Optimus at Fremont, Targets Scale in 2027

**Published: November 14, 2026**  
**Category: Robotics / Humanoids / Manufacturing**

![Futuristic humanoid robot](https://cdn.pixabay.com/photo/2023/05/24/17/49/ai-generated-8015427_640.jpg)

*Image: AI-generated humanoid robot from Pixabay (Pixabay Content License)*

Tesla confirmed in its Q3 2026 update (released October 21, 2026) that production of production-quality Optimus humanoid robots has begun on high-volume lines at its Fremont, California factory. The company described these as the first production-quality units manufactured and stated that the current process is suitable for a commercial product, while continuing hardware and manufacturing iterations.

## Production Status and Timeline

Elon Musk described the effort as early production during the earnings call. Most equipment for a line designed around one million units annually is installed in Fremont (space previously used for Model S/X). Reaching an annualized rate of approximately 20,000 units per week is expected to take at least a year, possibly longer, due to the complexity of a new product category (roughly 10,000 components that must align).

External customer deliveries are targeted for around the end of 2027, contingent on further AI, reliability, and durability improvements. A second site in Texas is under construction. Tesla has not disclosed exact unit counts produced so far, pricing, or specific external customers.

## Challenges and Progress

Musk has noted ongoing work on hands, AI for open-ended tasks, and long-duration reliability. The ramp is expected to follow an S-curve with a slow, bumpy start. The claim of a commercial-suitable process is a manufacturing milestone rather than proof of full autonomous utility at scale.

This sits within a broader wave of humanoid activity: large funding rounds, factory pilots (e.g., Gemini Robotics on Mercedes lines), and competing production plans from firms in China, Europe, and the US.

## Broader Context

Humanoid production remains early across the industry. Tesla's vertical integration (manufacturing expertise, AI chips, and data) is viewed as a potential advantage, though execution risks around reliability and cost are significant. Industry observers continue to distinguish between starting production lines and achieving useful, high-uptime deployments.

Updates on unit output, task performance metrics, or partnership announcements will be key signals in the coming quarters.
