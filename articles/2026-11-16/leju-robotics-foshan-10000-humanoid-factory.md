# Leju Robotics’ Foshan Plant Targets 10,000 Humanoid Robots a Year as CNN Gains Rare Access

![Futuristic humanoid robot illustration](https://cdn.pixabay.com/photo/2024/03/22/06/06/ai-generated-8649013_640.png)

*Image from Pixabay under the Pixabay Content License. Artist: Enio-ia.*

CNN has obtained rare access to Leju Robotics’ joint-venture factory in Foshan, Guangdong, which aims to produce up to 10,000 humanoid robots annually. The facility, operated with Dongfang Precision Group under the DF Primo name, launched earlier in 2026 and represents one of China’s most ambitious efforts to industrialize humanoid production.

## Factory Scale and Capabilities

- **Capacity and size**: Approximately 15,000 square meters. Target of 10,000 units per year. Reports from the plant’s earlier opening phase indicated the ability to complete a robot roughly every 30 minutes on an automated line with 24 precision assembly processes and dozens of inspection points.
- **Models**: Multiple humanoid variants, including wheeled designs (such as the Huavo 5 W) optimized for industrial environments and bipedal models. Over 1,000 components per unit; consistency of assembly is cited as the primary engineering challenge.
- **Process**: Modular assembly stations, AGVs for parts movement, automated weighing and verification of components, and extensive quality checks (gait, stability, range-of-motion) before shipment. Software and end-effector swaps allow relatively rapid model changes.
- **Partnership**: Leju provides designs and expertise; Dongfang Precision (historically strong in packaging equipment) handles manufacturing scale. The venture benefits from Guangdong’s dense industrial robotics supply chain.

CNN’s November 15–16, 2026 reporting is the first major Western media access to the production floor. Engineering director Huhehanggai emphasized that producing one robot is straightforward; producing many that are consistent and reliable is the real challenge.

## Context in the Humanoid Race

China is rapidly scaling humanoid manufacturing. Multiple firms (including AGIBOT, UBTech, and others) have announced multi-thousand-unit targets. Leju’s Foshan plant is notable for its partnership structure, automated processes, and public visibility via the CNN visit. Deliveries have already been reported to companies such as FAW Hongqi, Nio, and Haier in some accounts.

Western efforts (Tesla Optimus, Figure, Apptronik, Boston Dynamics) remain more focused on capability demonstrations and smaller-scale production. Chinese manufacturers are betting that high-volume, lower-cost units combined with improving foundation models will win industrial and service deployments first.

## Helpful Tips

- Distinguish between announced capacity and actual shipped volume; humanoid factories often ramp more slowly than headline targets.
- Wheeled vs. bipedal variants serve different use cases — wheeled models are generally easier to stabilize for factory or warehouse tasks.
- Watch for software and foundation-model integration announcements; hardware scale alone does not guarantee useful autonomous behavior.
- Component standardization (actuators, sensors, batteries) remains a bottleneck across the industry; suppliers in Guangdong are accelerating.
- For comparison, track Tesla Optimus, Figure, and Apptronik production updates — the gap between Chinese volume ambitions and Western deployment timelines is a key industry story.
