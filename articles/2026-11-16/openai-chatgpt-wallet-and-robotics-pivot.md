# OpenAI’s Dual Move: ChatGPT Wallet for Agents and Folding the Robotics Team Into General Models

In mid-November 2026 OpenAI made two closely watched moves that together signal how the company sees the future of AI agents: they should handle more of everyday digital life (including payments) while also becoming capable enough to control physical robots without specialized stacks.

### ChatGPT Wallet: Secure Agentic Purchases
On November 13, OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet. It lets the company’s always-on agents (“dots”) complete purchases on a user’s behalf. Built in partnership with American Express, Mastercard, and Visa, the system is designed so agents never see the user’s actual card details. Instead, after the user reviews the items, total, and selected card and approves with a biometric check (Face ID, fingerprint, etc.), the payment network issues a one-time payment token.

Key safeguards include:
- One-time addition of the card; no re-entry of details for later purchases.
- Explicit user approval and security check required for every transaction.
- Preservation of existing card rewards and dispute protections.
- Limited initial rollout to U.S. ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18+ with eligible U.S.-issued cards, accessed via dots rather than ordinary ChatGPT chats.

OpenAI has stated that over time users may set parameters (spending limits, categories, time windows) so agents can act with less frequent approval, while still keeping the user in control.

### Robotics Team Reorganization
Around the same time, OpenAI reorganized (and effectively dissolved the standalone structure of) its robotics team. Researchers moved into the main research organization under Chief Research Officer Mark Chen; applied robotics and hardware staff moved into the broader Applied organization under President Greg Brockman. The company frames this as acceleration, not retreat: general-purpose frontier models such as GPT-6 Astra have demonstrated they can control robots directly.

Supporting evidence cited includes an Nvidia study in which a GPT-6 Astra-powered agent achieved a 74.8% success rate on a simulated manipulation benchmark (RoboLab-120) versus 51.0% for the best specialized robotics models. OpenAI maintains that robotics remains part of its long-term vision and that CEO Sam Altman still targets a public humanoid demonstration in 2027. This is the second time OpenAI has restructured robotics efforts (the first standalone team was wound down in 2020).

### Connecting the Dots
Both moves reflect a bet on generalist agents. A model that can reason, call tools, manage a wallet with user approval, and issue motor commands to a robot is more powerful than separate specialized systems. Challenges remain: real-world robot performance still lags simulation, token costs for high-frequency control can be high, and payment systems must navigate fraud detection and merchant compatibility. Nonetheless, the combination of secure agentic commerce and general-model robotics control points toward AI systems that act across both digital and physical domains.

**Helpful Tip**: When testing agentic payment features, start with low-value, reversible purchases and carefully review approval prompts until you understand the exact data flow and token handling.
