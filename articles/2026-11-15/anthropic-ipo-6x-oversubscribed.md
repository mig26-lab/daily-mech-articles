# Anthropic IPO Order Book Six Times Oversubscribed Amid CEO’s Compute Uncertainty Warnings

**Published: November 15, 2026**  
**Category: AI / Technology / Business**

Anthropic’s blockbuster IPO has drawn extraordinary investor demand, with the order book reportedly covered roughly six times over available shares. The offering of 825 million Class A shares at $70–$78 each could raise up to $64.4 billion, implying a fully diluted valuation of around $1.4 trillion, with trading expected to begin on the Nasdaq as ANT on or around November 19, 2026. A concurrent $26 billion private placement with strategic investors including Google and Nvidia further bolsters the capital raise.

## Demand and Structure

Bloomberg and other reports indicate orders exceed available shares by about six times, reflecting intense appetite for exposure to the Claude maker. Pricing is anticipated around November 18, with retail allocation potentially reaching 20%. The dual-class structure and Long-Term Benefit Trust limit pure financial shareholder influence, preserving Anthropic’s safety-oriented governance.

Run-rate revenue has climbed to approximately $81 billion as of late October, up sharply from $71 billion in September, driven by enterprise adoption of Claude models for coding, analysis, and agentic tasks. However, the company faces massive compute commitments—reportedly around $900.9 billion in signed spend, largely for infrastructure—with significant near-term obligations.

## Leadership Comments and Risks

At a JPMorgan investor event, CEO Dario Amodei emphasized the uncertainty ahead: “There’s a huge uncertainty in what’s going to happen next year.” He described compute capacity planning as “the deepest business dilemma that the company faces,” noting that capacity must be secured one to two years in advance without full visibility into future revenue. These remarks underscore the capital intensity of frontier AI, even as demand for the IPO surges.

Nvidia’s $10 billion commitment through the placement comes amid its own leadership transition following Jensen Huang’s resignation. Broader market context includes questions about AI debt levels, with Goldman Sachs noting AI-related debt approaching $687 billion year-to-date.

## Implications for the Sector

A successful debut could validate the AI investment thesis and provide liquidity for the ecosystem. Weakness, however, might amplify caution amid Nvidia’s turbulence and ongoing debates over sustainability of high valuations. The six-times coverage signals strong institutional and retail interest, but execution on growth amid compute constraints will determine long-term performance.

## Looking Ahead

Investors will watch the final pricing, first-day trading, and subsequent disclosures on unit economics. Anthropic’s emphasis on constitutional AI differentiates it, yet competition from OpenAI, Google, and others remains fierce. The IPO marks a pivotal moment where research narrative meets public-market scrutiny.
