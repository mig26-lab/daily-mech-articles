# Zipline Raises $1.5 Billion to Scale Nationwide Drone Delivery Network

**Published: November 15, 2026**  
**Category: Robotics / Logistics / Technology**

On November 5, 2026, Zipline announced a $1.5 billion funding round that values the autonomous drone delivery company at $21.5 billion post-money. The round, led by Paradigm, WCM Investment Management, and Layer Global, includes participation from Valor Equity Partners, Fidelity, Uber, Tiger Global, J.P. Morgan, ARK Invest, and Salesforce Ventures. The capital is earmarked for expanding U.S. manufacturing capacity, hiring, and building a nationwide drone delivery network.

The same day, Zipline and Walmart signed a new multi-year agreement to add 300 drone delivery sites across the United States, a significant expansion from the 21 sites currently operating in Texas and Arkansas. Uber has also set a target of one million daily deliveries with Zipline by 2029.

## From Medical Deliveries in Africa to U.S. Scale

Zipline began commercial operations in Rwanda in 2016, delivering blood and medical supplies to remote clinics. It later expanded across Ghana, Nigeria, Kenya, and Côte d’Ivoire before scaling in the United States. The company has logged more than 150 million commercial autonomous miles without a safety incident and claims its medical deliveries help save thousands of lives annually.

U.S. regulatory barriers once limited beyond-visual-line-of-sight (BVLOS) operations, pushing early growth overseas. Improvements in FAA frameworks and Zipline’s track record have enabled faster domestic expansion. The company notes that U.S. flight volumes grew roughly 100x over the last 18 months in some metrics, with Dallas-Fort Worth alone exceeding one million deliveries.

## Technology and Operations

Zipline’s system includes long-range fixed-wing aircraft that drop packages via parachute or precision platforms, plus shorter-range VTOL designs for denser areas. Supporting infrastructure covers launch and landing systems, logistics software, and manufacturing. The company has developed much of its supply chain internally because mature drone-delivery ecosystems did not previously exist.

Partner results show measurable impact. Some long-standing restaurant partners saw delivery sales rise 26% between March and October 2026, with Zipline orders accounting for a large share of the increase. Median October order times in certain metros were reported around 19 minutes from checkout versus 30-plus minutes for ground delivery. Zipline also states its aircraft are significantly quieter than competing systems.

## Broader Implications for Robotics and Logistics

Drone delivery remains one of the more mature commercial applications of autonomous robotics outside controlled factories. Scaling to hundreds of Walmart sites and high-volume Uber targets tests both technology reliability and regulatory, noise, and community acceptance hurdles. Success could accelerate similar deployments in medical, retail, and last-mile logistics.

Competition is intensifying. Amazon continues expanding its own drone program, while other startups pursue different architectures. Zipline’s combination of proven international safety record, manufacturing capacity (reportedly capable of high annual aircraft output), and large commercial partnerships positions it as a leading player in physical AI for logistics.

## Looking Ahead

The $1.5 billion raise nearly triples Zipline’s valuation from roughly $7.6 billion earlier in 2026. Execution will depend on manufacturing ramp-up, site permitting, and sustained operational performance at higher volumes. For the robotics sector, the round underscores investor confidence that autonomous delivery systems can move from niche pilots to nationwide infrastructure.
