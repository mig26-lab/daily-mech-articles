# Gemini Robotics 2.5 Delivers Higher Success Rates on Factory Floors

**Published: November 15, 2026**  
**Category: Robotics / AI / Physical AI**

![Futuristic humanoid robot in city environment](https://cdn.pixabay.com/video/2025/04/07/270317_tiny.jpg)

*Image: Futuristic robotics scene from Pixabay (Pixabay Content License)*

Google DeepMind released Gemini Robotics 2.5 in mid-November 2026, describing it as its most capable vision-language-action model to date. The system is designed to serve as an “AI brain” for robots operating in dynamic, unstructured environments alongside humans. In real-world testing with Apptronik’s Apollo 2 humanoid on Mercedes-Benz production lines, the model achieved a 96% success rate packing automotive parts. Across more than 200 distinct tasks, success rates improved 30–100% depending on the task, while execution times dropped 20–30% versus the prior generation.

## Technical Advances

Gemini Robotics 2.5 builds on the Gemini 4 family (including Argon) and improves dexterity with everyday tools, reliability near obstacles and people, and overall physical steerability. It is natively compatible with multiple platforms, including Apptronik Apollo 2 and Franka Duo grippers. Partners such as Agile Robots and Boston Dynamics are also integrating the model. A new open benchmark for testing against jailbreaks was released alongside the model to support safety evaluation.

Apptronik CEO Jeff Cardenas noted that post-training improvements carried over to production environments with minimal drop-off, highlighting the practical transfer from lab to factory. The results reinforce the broader trend of foundation models moving from pure digital tasks into physical control.

## Broader Robotics Landscape

The release coincides with other activity: OpenAI has disbanded its dedicated robotics team in favor of applying general-purpose models to robot control; Tesla continues targeting Optimus deployments for practical factory work by 2027; multiple Chinese and European humanoids are logging hours in convenience stores, warehouses, and inspection roles. Vision-language-action models like Gemini Robotics 2.5, Runway’s Praxis-1 (open weights), and NVIDIA GR00T 2 are reducing the need for task-specific programming and accelerating deployment.

Challenges remain. Success rates still vary by task, long-horizon reliability is incomplete, and safety around humans requires ongoing evaluation. Energy, cost, and mechanical durability of the robot bodies themselves are separate constraints from the AI brain.

## Outlook

Gemini Robotics 2.5 demonstrates measurable progress toward useful industrial work. As more partners integrate the model and as simulation-to-real transfer improves, expect wider pilots in automotive, logistics, and electronics assembly through 2027. The combination of capable VLA models with maturing humanoid hardware is one of the clearer near-term paths for physical AI to leave the lab.
