# GXO Logistics Signs Letter of Intent for Up to 1,000 Apptronik Apollo Humanoid Robots

**Published: November 15, 2026**  
**Category: Robotics / Logistics / Physical AI**

![Humanoid robot illustration](https://cdn.pixabay.com/photo/2024/10/24/16/17/ai-generated-9146148_640.png)

*Image: Humanoid robot from Pixabay (Pixabay Content License)*

GXO Logistics, the world’s largest pure-play contract logistics provider, has signed a letter of intent with Apptronik to expand trials of the Apollo humanoid robot and potentially deploy up to 1,000 units globally over four years. Announced on November 12, 2026, the agreement marks one of the largest conditional commitments to humanoid robots in the warehouse sector to date.

## Deal Structure and Timeline

The LOI is not a firm purchase order. Expanded live trials are scheduled to begin in 2027 across multiple warehouse workflows, including goods-to-person and person-to-goods picking, case and tote movement, packing, and sortation. If the robots meet jointly agreed performance, safety, and operational key performance indicators (KPIs), the companies intend to move toward commercial deployment under a Robotics-as-a-Service (RaaS) model. Initial North American deployments are targeted for late 2027, with global scaling thereafter.

GXO CEO Patrick Kelleher has previously discussed cost targets in the $15–20 per hour range (including operating costs) for humanoids to achieve return on investment, noting that robot operating costs excluding capital could fall below $10 per hour within two years as prices decline. GXO already operates more than 45 humanoid pilots across vendors and expects nearly 20,000 robots of various types across its network by the end of 2026.

## Building on Prior Collaboration

The partnership builds on a 2024 proof-of-concept and a successful 2026 U.S. pilot focused on autonomous picking of small consumer goods. Apptronik and GXO have also conducted data collection and autonomous evaluations on more than 50 logistics tasks at Apptronik’s Robot Park facility. Apollo has previously been integrated with Google DeepMind’s Gemini Robotics models in other industrial settings, such as automotive parts kitting.

GXO operates more than 1,000 facilities totaling over 200 million square feet and employs more than 150,000 people. The company has been one of the most active testers of humanoid platforms, evaluating systems from Apptronik, Agility Robotics, and others as it seeks flexible automation that can work alongside existing equipment and human teams.

## Implications for Logistics Automation

Humanoid form factors offer potential advantages in warehouses designed for people: they can navigate existing aisles, handle varied items, and switch between tasks without extensive facility redesign. The RaaS model reduces upfront capital requirements for operators, aligning robot costs more closely with labor expenses. Success will depend on consistent reliability in unstructured environments, battery endurance across shifts, and safe collaboration with human workers.

This LOI follows other recent humanoid activity, including Tesla’s early production-quality Optimus units, AGIBOT store trials in China, and Swiss Embodied AI’s AIR wheeled platform. Together they illustrate accelerating movement from pilots toward conditional commercial pathways.

## Challenges and Next Steps

The conditional nature of the agreement underscores remaining technical and economic hurdles. Dexterous manipulation of deformable or irregular objects, recovery from errors, and sustained uptime in live operations will be rigorously tested during the 2027 trials. Regulatory and labor considerations around shared workspaces will also factor into scaling decisions.

As of mid-November 2026, the GXO-Apptronik LOI represents a significant signal of commercial intent in physical AI for logistics. Results from the expanded trials will provide concrete data on whether humanoids can deliver reliable, cost-effective work at warehouse scale.
