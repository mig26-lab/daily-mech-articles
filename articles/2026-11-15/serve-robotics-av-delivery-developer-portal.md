# Serve Robotics Opens Autonomous Delivery Network to Developers and Businesses via Public API

**Published: November 15, 2026**  
**Category: Robotics / Delivery / Physical AI**

On November 15, 2026, Serve Robotics launched the AV Delivery Developer Portal, giving developers, businesses, hobbyists, and AI assistants programmatic access to its fleet of autonomous sidewalk delivery robots. This marks a shift from exclusive partnerships with large delivery platforms to open infrastructure for last-mile logistics.

## What the Portal and API Enable

The portal includes a public API and a sandbox environment. Developers can request pickups and drop-offs, confirm details, and track robots in real time. Serve handles dispatch, routing, navigation, and remote supervision. The sandbox lets users simulate full workflows and test edge cases before requesting live robot access, which requires company approval.

Documentation is available at developer.serverobotics.com. Deliveries are limited to active service areas, operating hours, and items that fit the robots’ cargo compartments. All robots include built-in safety systems and human oversight.

CEO and co-founder Ali Kashani stated: “You shouldn’t have to start a robotics company to build something useful with robots. We’ve spent years figuring out how robots can navigate neighborhoods. Opening our fleet gives us an opportunity to serve needs we haven’t thought of.”

## Scale and Background

Serve’s robots have completed hundreds of thousands of deliveries across more than 150 neighborhoods in 20 U.S. cities. Previously available mainly through third-party partners, the fleet can now support custom applications such as office-to-office transport, merchant-to-customer deliveries, or integrations with AI agents that trigger physical tasks.

This follows the November 5 launch of Serve Physical AI Services, which opened underlying robot technology to companies building new platforms. The new portal extends that openness to the operational fleet itself.

## Implications for Last-Mile Delivery

By treating sidewalk robots as an accessible API resource, Serve lowers the barrier for innovation in autonomous logistics. Local businesses can expand delivery capacity without hiring drivers, while developers can embed physical actions into software workflows. Partnerships like the one with NoScrubs for laundry in Los Angeles already demonstrate non-food use cases.

The approach aligns with broader physical AI trends where foundation models and agents interact with real-world systems rather than remaining purely digital.

## Key Takeaways

- Public API and sandbox for summoning Serve’s sidewalk robots.
- Live access requires approval; sandbox is open for testing.
- Hundreds of thousands of prior deliveries across 20 U.S. cities.
- Enables custom business and AI-agent integrations beyond traditional platforms.

## Helpful Tips

- Start in the sandbox to map workflows and error handling before requesting live robots.
- Design payloads around the robots’ cargo limits and service-area constraints.
- Explore AI assistant integrations so agents can dispatch physical deliveries.
- Monitor Serve’s service maps, as coverage continues to expand.
