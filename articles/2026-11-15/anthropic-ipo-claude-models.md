# Anthropic Prepares Historic IPO as Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 Push the Frontier

**Published: November 15, 2026**  
**Category: AI / Technology / Business**

Anthropic is on the cusp of one of the most anticipated initial public offerings in technology history. The company behind the Claude family of AI models has set a price range of $70 to $78 per share for 825 million Class A shares, aiming for a fully diluted valuation of up to approximately $1.404 trillion. Trading is expected to begin on the Nasdaq around November 19, 2026, under the ticker symbol ANT. This comes amid a broader market reevaluation of AI valuations and follows the release of its latest models, Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2.

## The IPO Structure and Financial Picture

The offering is expected to raise between $57.75 billion and $64.35 billion from the public sale alone. In addition, Anthropic has arranged a $26 billion private placement of non-voting Class N stock with cornerstone investors including Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft, and TSMC. Google and Nvidia are each contributing around $10 billion. Net proceeds to the company could reach $84.6 billion to $93.7 billion if underwriters exercise their overallotment option.

On a non-diluted basis, the valuation sits closer to $1 trillion at the top of the range, with midpoint estimates around $968 billion at $74 per share. This is a step down from earlier speculative targets of $1.5–2 trillion but still represents a massive jump from the $965 billion private valuation in May 2026. Run-rate revenue has reportedly climbed to around $81 billion as of late October, underscoring rapid commercial adoption of Claude products in enterprise coding, analysis, and agentic workflows.

Governance remains distinctive: a Long-Term Benefit Trust is designed to elect a board majority, limiting the influence of pure Class A public shareholders. A portion of the offering is allocated for retail investors through brokers such as Charles Schwab, Fidelity, Robinhood, and others.

## Model Advances: Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2

Released just ahead of the IPO roadshow, Claude Fable 5.2 (generally available with safeguards) and Mythos 5.2 (for vetted security and life-sciences applications) have set new benchmarks. They outperform previous Opus 5.5, OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra, and Google’s Gemini 4 Carbon on many evaluations, often at lower cost per task and with up to 30% faster inference. Input pricing dropped to $8 per million tokens.

Standout demonstrations include long-horizon autonomous work: reports of 1,000 agents formalizing complex mathematical results in under 30 hours, solving open physics problems, and generating functional electronic designs from high-level prompts. Mythos 5.2 roughly doubled prior vulnerability discovery rates in certain codebases. The models also show increased self-awareness of evaluation contexts, a development noted by observers as a step toward more robust but still “jagged” general capabilities.

These releases coincide with broader enterprise tools, including Claude Meetings (beta for Team and Enterprise plans), which joins Google Meet calls as a participant for live note-taking and collaboration.

## Market Context and Risks

The IPO arrives against a backdrop of leadership turmoil at Nvidia (with Jensen Huang’s resignation) and ongoing questions about AI capital intensity, safety, and regulation. Anthropic’s emphasis on constitutional AI and safety research is both a differentiator and a potential constraint compared with more aggressive competitors. Success will depend on sustaining revenue growth amid intense competition from Google, OpenAI, Meta, and Chinese labs such as DeepSeek, while managing massive compute commitments.

Retail participation and the dual-class structure will be closely watched. If the debut is strong, it could re-energize the AI trade; weakness might amplify caution across the sector.

## Looking Ahead

Anthropic’s public listing will provide unprecedented transparency into the unit economics of frontier AI. Combined with the capability jumps in Fable and Mythos, it marks a maturation point for the industry—moving from pure research narrative to large-scale commercial and public-market reality.
