# South Korea Invests $330 Million to Localize Humanoid Robot Core Components, Targets 80% by 2030

**Published: November 15, 2026**  
**Category: Robotics / Technology / Industry**

![Humanoid robot in warehouse setting](https://cdn.pixabay.com/photo/2026/03/14/02/39/tungart7-robot-10172923_640.jpg)

*Image: Humanoid robot illustration from Pixabay (Pixabay Content License)*

South Korea’s government has approved a major investment of 440.2 billion won (approximately $330 million) to develop and localize core components for humanoid robots. The initiative, part of the Ultra-Innovative Economy Leading Project, focuses on actuators, dedicated batteries, and next-generation sensors, with a goal of achieving 80% localization of key parts by 2030. This positions South Korea, already the global leader in industrial robot density, to compete more effectively in the emerging humanoid market against Chinese suppliers.

## Investment Breakdown and Targets

The funding is allocated across three areas:
- Actuators: 100 billion won through 2029 to increase output from 35 Nm to 165 Nm (a fivefold improvement) and ensure durability exceeding 1 million cycles, with mass production targeted for 2029.
- Batteries: 271.4 billion won toward commercializing NCM and solid-state batteries supporting eight hours of continuous operation by 2030–2031.
- Sensors: 68.8 billion won for on-sensor AI semiconductors, with development through 2030.

Deputy Prime Minister and Finance Minister Lee Hyoung-il confirmed the plan at an emergency economic ministers’ meeting. The strategy aims to reduce reliance on overseas suppliers and build a domestic supply chain capable of supporting large-scale humanoid deployment.

## Context in Global Robot Density

The International Federation of Robotics reports South Korea at a record 1,066 industrial robots per 10,000 workers—more than double Singapore and far ahead of the global average of 152. This density underpins the country’s manufacturing strength and provides a foundation for humanoid expansion. Hyundai Motor Group and affiliates like Hyundai Mobis are already advancing actuator production for Boston Dynamics’ Atlas, with prototypes underway and plans for scaled capacity.

Private sector momentum includes supplier mobilization by Hyundai and investments in companies like Robotis, highlighted by JPMorgan as a promising humanoid component supplier.

## Strategic Implications

Localizing components addresses supply chain risks and cost pressures in humanoid production. Success could enable South Korea to capture a larger share of the physical AI market, supporting applications in manufacturing, logistics, and beyond. Challenges include scaling production quality, competing with established Chinese ecosystems, and integrating with global platforms.

## Looking Ahead

Mass production milestones in 2029 for actuators and commercialization of batteries by 2030 will be key indicators. Combined with private investments and existing robot density leadership, this government push could accelerate South Korea’s role in the humanoid robotics race.
