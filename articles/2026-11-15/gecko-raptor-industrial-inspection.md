# Gecko Robotics Unveils Raptor: Next-Generation Inspection Robot Speeds Critical Infrastructure Monitoring

**Published: November 15, 2026**  
**Category: Robotics / Industrial Technology**

![Industrial inspection robot climbing infrastructure](https://cdn.pixabay.com/photo/2024/10/24/16/17/ai-generated-9146148_640.png)

*Image: Robotics visualization from Pixabay (Pixabay Content License)*

Gecko Robotics has introduced Raptor, its latest wall-climbing industrial inspection robot, designed to dramatically accelerate data collection on pipelines, storage tanks, and other steel infrastructure. Unveiled at ADIPEC 2026, Raptor’s first major customer is ADNOC in the United Arab Emirates, where it supports efforts to integrate robotics and AI into predictive maintenance without taking facilities offline.

## Performance Leap

Raptor collects inspection data three to five times faster than Gecko’s previous generation of robots and up to 20 times faster than other automated tools currently on the market. Some company statements and reporting cite even larger gains—up to 50 times faster data collection relative to certain competitors—while capturing millions of data points per asset. This density of information feeds into Gecko’s AI-powered software platform (Cantilever) for structural analysis, digital twinning, and maintenance planning.

The robot uses advanced sensors and ultrasonic techniques to detect faults, corrosion, and weaknesses while assets remain in operation. Improved mobility includes the ability to perform car-like three-point turns, enabling navigation in confined and complex industrial environments. These capabilities reduce the need for scaffolding and human exposure to hazardous conditions, contributing to safer operations.

## ADNOC Partnership and Broader Impact

The ADNOC deployment builds on existing multi-year agreements. Prior contracts with ADNOC Gas and others have targeted hundreds of millions of dollars in value through reduced downtime, higher inspection coverage (previously reported at 99.6% greater coverage in pilots), and efficiency gains. The new Raptor rollout is expected to further lower costs—potentially by an order of magnitude in data collection—while increasing fidelity of assessments. This is particularly valuable for energy infrastructure resilience, including post-incident recovery scenarios in regions facing geopolitical risks.

Gecko has also secured significant contracts outside energy, including a reported $300 million agreement with the U.S. Navy for critical asset maintenance. The company is exploring local manufacturing in the UAE to meet demand.

## Industry Context

Specialized inspection and maintenance robots are maturing alongside humanoid platforms. While general-purpose humanoids attract headlines for factory kitting and retail trials, systems like Raptor address high-value, high-risk niches where precision, speed, and continuous operation deliver immediate ROI. Complementary developments include visual AI inspection startups raising capital (such as Tristar AI’s recent Series A) and pharmacy automation kiosks that fill prescriptions in seconds.

The combination of denser sensor data, faster robots, and AI analysis is shifting industrial maintenance from scheduled or reactive models toward continuous, predictive ones. This reduces unplanned outages, extends asset life, and improves safety metrics.

## Challenges and Outlook

Adoption still depends on integration with existing plant systems, regulatory acceptance in hazardous environments, and proving long-term reliability at scale. As more operators adopt these tools, the economics of industrial robotics improve further, creating a flywheel with AI model advances.

Raptor exemplifies how targeted robotics, rather than solely general-purpose humanoids, can deliver transformative results in the near term for critical infrastructure.
