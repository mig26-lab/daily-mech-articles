# NVIDIA Leadership Transition: Colette Kress Takes Interim Helm as AI Hardware Faces New Questions

On November 12, 2026, NVIDIA announced that co-founder and longtime CEO Jensen Huang had resigned effective immediately as President, CEO, and board member. Executive Vice President and Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress was named Interim CEO, while also retaining her CFO role. The board simultaneously appointed Harvey C. Jones as its first-ever Chair. The news, coming amid reports of personal matters involving Huang, sent NVIDIA shares lower and raised questions across the AI ecosystem about leadership continuity at the company that has powered much of the current generative AI boom.

## The Transition Details

NVIDIA’s official statement confirmed Huang’s departure after 33 years at the helm. Kress, who joined NVIDIA in 2013 after finance roles at Cisco and Microsoft, has been a steady public face for the company on earnings calls and investor communications. The board has begun a search for a permanent CEO, evaluating both internal and external candidates. Earnings for Q3 of fiscal 2027 are scheduled for November 17, a call that Kress will now lead.

Market reaction was immediate: shares opened sharply lower and closed down several percent, contributing to broader volatility in AI-related stocks. Analysts noted that while operational continuity is expected in the near term, the loss of Huang’s distinctive product vision and industry relationships introduces uncertainty into the GPU roadmap and strategic decisions around export controls and Chinese market access.

## Implications for the AI and Robotics Ecosystem

NVIDIA’s GPUs and software stack (CUDA, TensorRT, Isaac, GR00T) remain foundational for training frontier models and for robotics simulation and control. Recent releases such as GR00T 2 and partnerships with humanoid developers have reinforced the company’s role in physical AI. Any slowdown in roadmap execution or shifts in capital allocation under interim leadership could affect timelines for next-generation chips used by OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and a growing list of robotics firms.

Chinese labs’ reported use of restricted Blackwell-class chips has already heightened political scrutiny. Leadership change at NVIDIA may influence how aggressively the company navigates U.S. export policy and congressional interest in further restrictions. Customers are watching closely for signals on supply continuity and software support.

On the robotics side, NVIDIA’s Isaac Lab and related simulation tools have become standard for training embodied systems. Continued investment here under Kress’s leadership will be important for companies deploying humanoids in factories, logistics, and inspection.

## Looking Forward

Kress has emphasized commitment to customers, partners, developers, and shareholders, and to building on the foundation Huang established. The board’s appointment of an independent chair also introduces a new governance structure. Near-term focus will be on a clean earnings delivery and maintaining product momentum. Longer term, the choice of permanent CEO will shape whether NVIDIA prioritizes pure scale, software lock-in, open ecosystems, or further expansion into robotics and physical AI.

Investors and industry observers will track not only financial results but also any changes in tone around China, partnerships, and the pace of new hardware announcements.
