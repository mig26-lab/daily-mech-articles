# Swiss Startup Embodied AI Unveils AIR: A Wheeled Humanoid Designed to Learn Factory Tasks Beside Workers

**Published: November 15, 2026**  
**Category: Robotics / Manufacturing / Physical AI**

![Blue humanoid robot in factory](https://cdn.pixabay.com/photo/2024/03/20/03/06/ai-generated-8644499_640.jpg)

*Image: Futuristic robot illustration from Pixabay (Pixabay Content License)*

Lausanne-based Embodied AI, an EPFL spin-out, unveiled its AIR humanoid robot at Humanoid Europe 2026 in Munich on November 6. Developed in just nine months by a 25-person team, AIR is a wheeled platform with a humanoid upper body engineered for safe collaboration on factory floors without safety cages. The company reports it is already under trial with global manufacturers and has signed contracts with major OEMs while planning scaled production.

## Design Philosophy: Safety by Construction

Unlike many bipedal humanoids, AIR places heavy components—motors, gearboxes, and electronics—inside its torso. Its arms are light (approximately 2 kg each) and tendon-driven, so they carry minimal mass and yield on contact. Each arm can lift roughly 5 kg. The robot rides on a wheeled base because factory floors are flat and wheels provide greater stability and efficiency than legs. Safety derives primarily from physical compliance rather than software limits alone. AIR is designed to the ISO/TS 15066:2016 specification for collaborative robots, with CE marking planned for 2027 under the new EU Machinery Regulation.

This architecture allows AIR to share workspaces with human operators. Founder and CEO Francesco Stella (age 28), whose PhD research at EPFL informed the arm design, has emphasized that physical AI must solve safety to enter real factories at scale.

## Learning on the Job

AIR begins each new task under teleoperated human control, making it productive from the first shift. Every demonstration is recorded and used by the company’s AI stack—combining imitation learning with vision-language model orchestration—to refine policies. The company states that the robot typically achieves autonomous performance within about a month. Knowledge gained on one unit can transfer to others. In paid pilots the system has shown average task success rates around 95 percent on high-variation manual work such as kitting, packaging, picking, placing, and cable handling. One earlier two-arm cell evaluation reportedly reached 98 percent reliability against a 90 percent target.

## Company Background and Traction

Embodied AI was founded by Stella (EPFL PhD, prior MIT CSAIL research), CTO Kai Junge (Cambridge and EPFL), and COO Max Polzin (ETH Zurich and EPFL background). Backers include Faber VC (lead), Look AI Ventures, TechShop, Plug and Play, and Vento. The startup has highlighted that it reached an industrial humanoid and customer contracts with far lower capital intensity than many larger humanoid programs. AIR is intended for flexible manufacturing tasks that conventional caged robots struggle to automate and that currently rely on scarce human labor.

## Broader Context

AIR arrives amid a wave of humanoid activity: GXO’s conditional plan for up to 1,000 Apptronik Apollos, Tesla Optimus early production, AGIBOT retail trials, and advances in tactile sensing highlighted at Swiss Robotics Day. The wheeled, compliant design represents a pragmatic alternative to full bipedal platforms for structured industrial environments. Success will hinge on sustained reliability, rapid skill acquisition across diverse tasks, and cost structures that compete with human labor or specialized automation.

As of November 15, 2026, Embodied AI’s rapid path from research to signed OEM contracts illustrates how focused engineering and learning-from-demonstration approaches can accelerate practical physical AI deployment in manufacturing.
